Requirements-Driven Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture approach that starts from business, technical, compliance, availability, and cost requirements before selecting services.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Exam scenarios where multiple solutions are technically possible and the correct answer depends on constraints like budget, latency, RTO/RPO, compliance, or operational simplicity.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Ad hoc solution selection based only on preferred technology without validating business requirements.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Produces the most appropriate architecture for the actual business need, but requires careful analysis before implementation.
💰 Cost / Security
Define measurable targets such as uptime, RTO, RPO, latency, and data residency early. Security and compliance requirements must be treated as first-class design inputs, not later add-ons.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company asks for a new architecture for a customer-facing platform. They mention low cost, EU data residency, and recovery from regional failures within one hour.
Start with requirements-driven design and choose services and topology that satisfy cost, residency, and recovery objectives rather than selecting products first.
Managed Services First Architecture Design

What is it?

Design principle that favors fully managed Google Cloud services over self-managed infrastructure whenever they satisfy the workload requirements.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Teams that want to reduce patching, scaling, failover, backups, and operational burden while accelerating delivery.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Workloads requiring OS-level control, unsupported software, custom kernel modules, hardware-specific licensing, or deep platform customization.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Lower operational overhead and faster delivery vs. reduced low-level control and customization.
💰 Cost / Security
Managed services often reduce hidden operating cost and lower misconfiguration risk. Use IAM, CMEK where required, and private connectivity options to keep managed services aligned with enterprise security.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company can run its application either on self-managed VMs or on a managed serverless platform, and there is no requirement for OS-level access.
Choose the managed service first because it meets the requirement with less operational overhead.
Regional Architecture Architecture Design

What is it?

Application design pattern where resources are deployed in one Google Cloud region, typically across multiple zones for higher availability.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Workloads serving one geography, applications with moderate disaster recovery requirements, and systems where lower cost and simpler operations are more important than surviving a full regional outage automatically.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Mission-critical global systems requiring continued service during regional failures with minimal disruption.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Simpler and less expensive than multi-region architecture, but cannot fully protect against a complete regional outage by itself.
💰 Cost / Security
Use multiple zones within the region to remove zonal single points of failure. Store backups or replicated data in another region if regional disaster recovery is required.
💡 Exam Scenario
An internal business application must survive zonal failures, but the business accepts several hours of downtime in the event of a regional disaster.
Use a regional architecture spread across multiple zones, with cross-region backups for disaster recovery.
Multi-Regional Architecture Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture pattern that places application components and/or data across multiple regions to improve resilience, global reach, and fault tolerance.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Business-critical applications with strict uptime targets, global user bases, and low RTO/RPO requirements.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Small internal systems, development environments, or cost-sensitive workloads without strong cross-region availability requirements.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Strong resilience and lower user latency globally vs. higher complexity, replication challenges, and increased cost.
💰 Cost / Security
Replicate only critical tiers and datasets to control cost. Ensure IAM, secrets, encryption, and organization policies are applied consistently across all regions.
💡 Exam Scenario
A retail platform must continue serving users even if an entire region becomes unavailable.
Use a multi-regional architecture with global traffic distribution and replicated services or data stores that support regional failover.
Active-Active Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Resilience pattern where multiple environments or regions simultaneously serve production traffic.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Low-latency global applications, very high availability requirements, and services where fast failover is essential.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Simple workloads with limited budget, architectures with difficult write-conflict handling, or applications that do not justify the operational complexity.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Fast failover and better utilization of deployed capacity vs. more complex replication, consistency, testing, and traffic management.
💰 Cost / Security
Requires duplicated running capacity, which increases cost. Validate identity, policy, logging, and secrets configuration consistently in every active environment.
💡 Exam Scenario
A payment platform must stay online during a regional outage and continue serving users from another region with minimal failover delay.
Use an active-active multi-region architecture with globally distributed traffic routing and replicated backend services.
Active-Passive Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Resilience pattern where one environment actively serves traffic and another standby environment is used only during failover.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Disaster recovery architectures where lower cost is preferred over instant failover, and where short recovery time is acceptable.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Applications requiring near-zero failover time or constant global low-latency access from multiple regions.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Lower running cost and simpler data consistency than active-active, but failover takes longer and standby resources may be underutilized.
💰 Cost / Security
Use automation for failover and regular testing to avoid broken standby systems. Backups, IAM, and secrets must be maintained in the passive environment as well.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company wants disaster recovery in another region but needs to minimize steady-state cost and can tolerate a short failover process.
Use an active-passive architecture with a warm or cold standby in a secondary region.
Stateless Application Tier Architecture Design

What is it?

Design approach where application instances do not keep session or business state on local disk or in-memory only on one node.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Web tiers, APIs, microservices, and autoscaled services behind load balancers.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Legacy applications that require sticky local state and cannot externalize sessions or shared state.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Easier scaling, replacement, and recovery vs. the need for external state stores such as databases, caches, or object storage.
💰 Cost / Security
Externalize state to managed storage or cache services and protect those systems with IAM and network controls. Stateless design reduces recovery time because failed instances can be recreated safely.
💡 Exam Scenario
A web application behind a load balancer must auto-scale rapidly across zones without breaking user sessions.
Make the application tier stateless and store session data in an external shared service.
Loose Coupling with Pub/Sub Architecture Design

What is it?

Event-driven architecture pattern where producers and consumers are decoupled through asynchronous messaging using Pub/Sub.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Systems that need elastic scaling, independent service evolution, workload buffering, and resilience against temporary downstream failures.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Strict synchronous request-response workflows where the caller requires an immediate transactional result.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better resilience and scalability vs. added complexity in event handling, retries, and eventual consistency.
💰 Cost / Security
Pub/Sub can absorb traffic spikes and smooth backend load, reducing overprovisioning cost. Restrict publishers and subscribers with least-privilege IAM roles.
💡 Exam Scenario
Orders arrive unpredictably and downstream inventory and billing services must process them independently without losing messages during spikes.
Use Pub/Sub to decouple producers from consumers and allow asynchronous scaling.
Caching Layer Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture pattern that stores frequently accessed data closer to the application or user to reduce latency and backend load.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Read-heavy workloads, repeated queries, session storage, and high-traffic web applications needing faster response times.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Highly volatile data that changes constantly and cannot tolerate stale reads, or workloads with minimal read reuse.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better latency and reduced backend load vs. cache invalidation complexity and potential stale data.
💰 Cost / Security
Proper caching can reduce database or compute cost significantly. Protect cache access through private networking and avoid storing sensitive data unless the security model is clear.
💡 Exam Scenario
A product catalog application receives heavy repeated read traffic and the database is becoming a bottleneck.
Add a caching layer such as Memorystore to reduce repeated database reads and improve response time.
Global External HTTP(S) Load Balancing Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture choice that uses Google’s global anycast HTTP(S) load balancing to distribute user traffic to the best available backends.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Global web applications, multi-region frontends, path-based routing, SSL termination at the edge, and Cloud CDN integration.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Internal-only services, non-HTTP workloads, or small single-region apps that do not need global traffic steering.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Excellent global routing and edge capabilities vs. more configuration complexity than a simple regional setup.
💰 Cost / Security
Use managed certificates, Cloud Armor, and Cloud CDN where appropriate. Centralized load balancing reduces exposure by standardizing frontend security controls.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company serves users worldwide and wants traffic sent to the nearest healthy region using one public IP and one HTTPS endpoint.
Use the global external HTTP(S) load balancer with multi-region backends.
Queue-Based Buffering Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture pattern that places a durable queue or messaging system between producers and workers to smooth bursts and protect downstream services.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Batch processing, asynchronous tasks, bursty ingestion pipelines, and systems where producers generate work faster than consumers can process it at peak times.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Real-time synchronous transactions that require immediate end-user confirmation from all downstream systems.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Improves durability and absorbs spikes vs. increased end-to-end processing latency and eventual consistency.
💰 Cost / Security
Buffering lets worker fleets scale based on queue depth, which reduces overprovisioning cost. Secure producers and consumers with IAM and private service communication where possible.
💡 Exam Scenario
An image-processing system receives large unpredictable upload spikes and workers fail when traffic arrives too fast.
Introduce a queue or Pub/Sub-based buffering layer so workers process tasks asynchronously at a controlled rate.
Data Store Selection by Access Pattern Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture decision process that chooses storage and database technologies based on workload behavior such as OLTP, analytics, key-value, time-series, or object storage.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Exam questions where several storage services are possible and the correct answer depends on consistency, scale, query type, mutation pattern, and latency.
❌ Not a Good Fit
One-size-fits-all thinking where a familiar database is used for every workload regardless of its access pattern.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Best performance and cost efficiency when matched correctly, but requires careful understanding of workload characteristics.
💰 Cost / Security
Choosing the wrong database increases both cost and operational complexity. Apply least privilege, encryption, backup, and residency controls according to the selected store.
💡 Exam Scenario
A team wants one system for time-series sensor ingestion, another for global relational transactions, and another for ad hoc analytics on historical data.
Match services to patterns: Bigtable for high-scale time-series, Spanner for global relational transactions, and BigQuery for analytical queries.
RTO/RPO-Based Disaster Recovery Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Disaster recovery planning approach that selects backup, replication, and failover strategies according to target recovery time and recovery point objectives.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Critical systems where business impact determines how much downtime and data loss are acceptable.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Architectures chosen without defined recovery targets, or workloads where no one has clarified acceptable outage and data loss thresholds.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Lower RTO and RPO require more automation, replication, and cost; relaxed targets allow cheaper and simpler solutions.
💰 Cost / Security
Align spending with business impact instead of overbuilding every system. Protect backups with IAM, retention policies, encryption, and separation from the primary failure domain.
💡 Exam Scenario
One application can tolerate 24 hours of recovery and some data loss, while another must recover in minutes with almost no data loss.
Design separate DR strategies based on each system’s RTO and RPO rather than applying one identical architecture to both.
Backup and Restore Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Protection strategy that focuses on creating recoverable copies of data and systems to restore service after corruption, deletion, or disaster.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Workloads needing recovery from operator error, ransomware, accidental deletion, or application-level corruption.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Treating high availability alone as a replacement for backups.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Simpler and less expensive than full multi-region hot redundancy, but restoration takes time and may lose recent data depending on backup frequency.
💰 Cost / Security
Use lifecycle policies, versioning, and backup retention aligned to compliance and business needs. Store backups outside the primary failure domain and control restore permissions carefully.
💡 Exam Scenario
A database is highly available across zones, but the company also needs recovery from accidental table deletion from the previous day.
Implement backup and restore design because HA does not protect against logical deletion or corruption.
Least-Privilege IAM Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Security design principle that grants identities only the minimum permissions required to perform their tasks.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
All production architectures, especially environments with separation of duties, regulated data, or multiple teams.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Broad project-wide primitive roles granted for convenience, especially in production.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Stronger security and reduced blast radius vs. more effort in role design and access management.
💰 Cost / Security
Use predefined or custom roles scoped narrowly to the needed resource level. Least privilege reduces the impact of credential theft, insider misuse, and accidental changes.
💡 Exam Scenario
A development team needs to deploy services but must not manage networking or access sensitive production data.
Grant narrowly scoped IAM roles aligned to deployment tasks, not broad owner or editor permissions.
Network Segmentation Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture approach that separates workloads by environment, sensitivity, or function using projects, VPC design, firewall rules, and service perimeters.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Enterprises with production and non-production isolation, sensitive data boundaries, and clear separation of duties.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Flat network designs where all workloads communicate freely without clear trust boundaries.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better security and governance vs. added design and operational complexity.
💰 Cost / Security
Segmenting environments reduces lateral movement risk and supports compliance. Use Shared VPC, firewall policies, and VPC Service Controls where appropriate.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company wants central network governance, isolated production workloads, and protection against data exfiltration from managed services.
Use segmented project and network design with Shared VPC and VPC Service Controls where required.
Private Connectivity Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture principle of keeping service-to-service and hybrid connectivity on private paths instead of exposing workloads directly to the public internet.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Hybrid connectivity, private APIs, regulated workloads, internal services, and organizations minimizing internet exposure.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Architectures that unnecessarily expose internal systems publicly when private connectivity options exist.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Stronger security posture and reduced public attack surface vs. additional setup and possible higher connectivity cost.
💰 Cost / Security
Use private IPs, Private Google Access, Cloud VPN, Interconnect, and internal load balancing according to the requirement. Minimize public IP usage and enforce least-privilege firewall rules.
💡 Exam Scenario
Backend services and private VMs need to access Google APIs and on-premises systems without assigning public IPs.
Design with private connectivity options such as Private Google Access plus VPN or Interconnect as required.
Observability by Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture practice of embedding logging, monitoring, alerting, tracing, and SLO-oriented visibility into the system from the beginning.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Production systems requiring reliable operations, rapid troubleshooting, performance visibility, and service health tracking.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Waiting until after production incidents to add logs, metrics, or dashboards.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better operational insight and faster incident response vs. added setup work and potential telemetry cost if poorly controlled.
💰 Cost / Security
Collect the logs and metrics needed for operations and compliance, but avoid excessive noisy telemetry. Restrict access to observability data because logs can contain sensitive metadata.
💡 Exam Scenario
A business-critical application must alert operators when latency or error rates exceed service objectives and provide enough information to debug quickly.
Design observability into the architecture with Cloud Monitoring, Cloud Logging, alerting policies, and service-level objectives.
Cost-Optimized Architecture Architecture Design

What is it?

Design approach that meets functional and non-functional requirements with the lowest reasonable cost, without sacrificing required reliability, security, or performance.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Most exam questions that mention budget constraints, uncertain traffic, or the need to avoid overprovisioning.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Over-engineered architectures that add multi-region, always-on capacity, or self-managed components without clear business need.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Lower spend and simpler operations vs. potentially less headroom for extreme scenarios that are not part of the stated requirements.
💰 Cost / Security
Use autoscaling, storage tiering, serverless scale-to-zero, committed discounts for steady workloads, and Spot VMs for fault-tolerant jobs. Optimize for the stated requirement, not for hypothetical future needs.
💡 Exam Scenario
A startup needs a scalable public API with unpredictable traffic and wants to minimize idle infrastructure cost.
Choose a cost-optimized architecture such as serverless or autoscaled managed services instead of permanently provisioned infrastructure.
Performance-Optimized Architecture Architecture Design

What is it?

Design approach that prioritizes low latency, high throughput, and efficient resource usage by choosing the right compute, network, cache, and database patterns.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
User-facing apps with strict latency needs, large-scale data processing, HPC, and high-throughput transactional systems.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Cases where performance is moderate and the additional complexity or cost is not justified by business requirements.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better speed and throughput vs. potentially higher infrastructure cost and more specialized architecture decisions.
💰 Cost / Security
Place workloads near users or data, minimize cross-region transfers, and use caching or specialized databases where appropriate. Performance tuning must still respect IAM, encryption, and network security controls.
💡 Exam Scenario
A service is suffering from high latency because every request queries the primary database in another region and no caching layer exists.
Redesign for performance by colocating services appropriately and adding caching to reduce cross-region dependency and database load.
Compliance and Data Residency Design Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture approach that ensures data location, retention, access, and controls align with legal, industry, or organizational compliance requirements.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Regulated industries, region-restricted data, audit-heavy environments, and enterprise governance models.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Architectures that replicate or expose data across locations without checking residency or regulatory requirements.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better regulatory alignment and reduced compliance risk vs. reduced flexibility in service placement and architecture choices.
💰 Cost / Security
Select regions carefully, control replication, apply CMEK where required, and enforce access boundaries with IAM and organizational policies. Compliance needs can override cost or convenience preferences.
💡 Exam Scenario
A healthcare application must store and process sensitive records only in approved regions and maintain strict auditability.
Design for compliance and residency first by selecting approved regions and enforcing controlled access, logging, and encryption policies.
Migration with Minimal Refactoring Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture strategy that prioritizes fast migration of existing workloads with the least application change, often as a first phase before modernization.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Tight migration deadlines, legacy systems, risk-sensitive enterprises, and workloads that cannot be rewritten immediately.
❌ Not a Good Fit
New greenfield systems or cases where cloud-native redesign is practical and strongly beneficial from the start.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Faster migration with lower immediate risk vs. reduced long-term cloud optimization and potentially higher operating cost.
💰 Cost / Security
Lift-and-shift may preserve inefficiencies, so plan later optimization phases. Use secure landing zones, IAM, logging, and network controls even when applications are not yet modernized.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company must exit a data center quickly and move a legacy application to Google Cloud with as few code changes as possible.
Use a minimal-refactoring migration approach first, then optimize or modernize after stabilization.
Modernize to Cloud-Native Architecture Design

What is it?

Architecture strategy that redesigns applications to use managed, containerized, serverless, and event-driven cloud services for better agility and scalability.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Applications expected to evolve quickly, systems with variable traffic, and organizations seeking operational efficiency and faster feature delivery.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Legacy applications with tight deadlines, strict compatibility constraints, or no budget for redesign effort.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better scalability, agility, and operational efficiency vs. higher upfront redesign effort and migration complexity.
💰 Cost / Security
Cloud-native design often lowers long-term operations cost and improves resilience, but migration effort can be significant. Use secure-by-default managed services, least-privilege IAM, and observability from the beginning.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company is redesigning a monolith into independently deployable services that scale based on demand and minimize operational overhead.
Modernize to a cloud-native architecture using managed and loosely coupled services.
