Cloud Monitoring Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Managed monitoring service that collects metrics, tracks uptime, powers dashboards, and supports alerting for infrastructure and applications.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Service health monitoring; performance tracking; SLO and SLA measurement; dashboarding; resource utilization visibility; uptime checks.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Raw log retention and search without metrics use cases; archival-only requirements; teams that need monitoring but do not define meaningful service indicators.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Strong real-time operational visibility and alerting vs. dependence on well-defined metrics and thresholds to avoid noisy or low-value monitoring.
💰 Cost / Security
Build dashboards around business-critical systems and service-level indicators instead of collecting everything without purpose. Limit dashboard and metric access to authorized operators and support teams.
💡 Exam Scenario
A team needs dashboards for CPU, memory, latency, error rate, and uptime, plus automated notifications when service health degrades.
Use Cloud Monitoring to track metrics, define uptime checks, and create alerting policies.
Cloud Logging Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Managed centralized logging service that stores, searches, filters, and routes logs from Google Cloud resources and applications.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Troubleshooting production incidents; audit analysis; centralized application and infrastructure log collection; exporting logs to downstream systems.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Replacing structured metrics for latency or error threshold monitoring; keeping all logs forever without retention planning; treating logs as the only observability signal.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Detailed event visibility and searchable diagnostics vs. potential cost and noise if log volume is not controlled.
💰 Cost / Security
Create retention and export strategies so expensive or sensitive logs are stored appropriately. Protect logs carefully because they can contain IPs, metadata, stack traces, and operational details.
💡 Exam Scenario
Engineers need to investigate why a production service failed overnight and want to search application and infrastructure events from one place.
Use Cloud Logging for centralized log collection, filtering, and analysis.
Log-Based Metrics Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Custom metrics generated from log entries so operational events recorded in logs can drive dashboards and alerting.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Error-count alerts; application event thresholds; patterns that exist in logs but are not exposed as native metrics; operational tracking of specific log events.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Cases where a native platform metric already exists and is more direct; overly broad log parsing that creates noisy or high-cardinality metrics.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Converts rich log data into actionable metrics vs. requiring careful filter design to avoid false positives and unnecessary cost.
💰 Cost / Security
Use narrow filters and only create metrics for meaningful operational events. Avoid exposing sensitive payload details through labels or downstream dashboards.
💡 Exam Scenario
A team wants an alert whenever 404 responses exceed 5 percent of total requests in a short time window, but the signal exists in request logs.
Create a log-based metric and attach an alerting policy to it.
Alerting Policies Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Configurable rules in Cloud Monitoring that notify operators when metric, uptime, or log-based conditions meet defined thresholds.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Incident detection; service-level monitoring; operations response workflows; on-call notification for production systems.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Environments with no clear thresholds, no response owner, or alerts configured for every possible signal without prioritization.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Faster incident response and reduced downtime vs. alert fatigue if thresholds, grouping, or severity are poorly designed.
💰 Cost / Security
Alert only on actionable conditions with clear owners and escalation paths. Avoid exposing sensitive details in notification channels and incident messages.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company wants immediate notification when API error rate or latency exceeds acceptable production thresholds.
Configure alerting policies in Cloud Monitoring using the appropriate metrics or log-based metrics.
Uptime Checks Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Monitoring probes that test the availability and responsiveness of public endpoints from multiple locations.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Public websites; APIs; externally reachable services; basic verification that a service is available to users.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Deep internal dependency diagnosis; private-only services without the required access path; replacing full application health instrumentation.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Simple external availability validation vs. limited insight into internal root cause.
💰 Cost / Security
Use uptime checks with alerting for critical public services. Be careful when exposing health endpoints so they reveal minimal sensitive information.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company wants to know quickly if its public website becomes unreachable from the internet.
Configure uptime checks and connect them to an alerting policy.
Dashboard-Based Operations Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Operational practice of combining metrics, charts, incidents, and service health indicators into dashboards for real-time visibility.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
NOC views; SRE workflows; executive service health summaries; platform operations; application troubleshooting.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Teams that rely only on raw logs or manual ad hoc inspection during incidents without prebuilt visibility.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Faster understanding of system state vs. time required to define meaningful and non-noisy visualizations.
💰 Cost / Security
Build dashboards around critical user journeys and infrastructure dependencies, not around vanity metrics. Restrict access because dashboards may expose internal topology or sensitive operational data.
💡 Exam Scenario
Operators need a single view showing service uptime, request rate, error rate, latency, and infrastructure health during incidents.
Use Cloud Monitoring dashboards to centralize operational visibility.
Centralized Log Routing Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Logging architecture pattern where logs are collected centrally and optionally exported to destinations such as analytics or archival systems.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Compliance retention; security analysis; organization-wide observability; long-term log storage; centralized operations teams.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Keeping all logs indefinitely in default storage without retention planning; assuming centralized collection alone replaces incident response workflows.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better governance, searchability, and downstream analytics vs. added design decisions around retention, routing, and storage cost.
💰 Cost / Security
Export long-term or high-volume logs to the appropriate destination to control cost. Apply access controls carefully because centralized logs may reveal activity across many systems.
💡 Exam Scenario
An enterprise wants centralized visibility for logs across projects and also wants to retain selected logs for longer-term analysis.
Use centralized log routing with exports to the appropriate storage or analytics destination.
Incident-Driven Monitoring Design Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Monitoring approach where metrics, logs, and alerts are designed around real production failure modes and customer-impacting symptoms.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Mature production systems; SRE-aligned environments; organizations wanting actionable monitoring instead of noisy telemetry collection.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Monitoring setups based only on default charts or collecting every possible metric without linking them to service impact.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Higher signal quality and better operational response vs. more upfront thought about service objectives and failure modes.
💰 Cost / Security
Focus on user-impacting indicators such as latency, availability, and error rate to reduce noise and spend. Monitoring design should avoid exposing confidential details while still enabling incident response.
💡 Exam Scenario
A team wants its observability platform to detect real customer-impacting issues instead of generating large volumes of low-value alerts.
Design monitoring around actionable service indicators and incident-driven alerting conditions.
Alert Fatigue Reduction Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Operational practice of reducing noisy, duplicate, low-priority, or unactionable alerts so on-call teams focus on important incidents.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Teams with growing systems, multiple alert sources, and on-call engineers suffering from too many notifications.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Environments that add alerts for every metric without ownership, prioritization, or tuning.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Fewer, higher-value incidents vs. the need for disciplined threshold tuning and continuous alert review.
💰 Cost / Security
Poor alert design wastes staff time and can cause real incidents to be missed. Group related alerts, use severity appropriately, and notify only the right responders.
💡 Exam Scenario
An operations team receives too many alerts, many of which are duplicates or non-actionable, and is missing important production problems.
Tune alerting policies to focus on actionable signals and reduce alert fatigue.
Structured Application Logging Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Practice of emitting application logs in structured fields rather than unstructured text so logs can be filtered, parsed, and analyzed more effectively.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Production services; microservices; systems where logs support troubleshooting, metric extraction, and cross-service correlation.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Free-form logging with inconsistent message formats that make filtering and automation difficult.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better searchability and automation vs. more discipline in application logging design.
💰 Cost / Security
Structured logs make it easier to build log-based metrics and incident filters, but sensitive data should never be logged unnecessarily. Standardize fields like severity, service name, request ID, and error code.
💡 Exam Scenario
A platform team wants reliable filters and log-based alerts for specific application failures across many services.
Implement structured application logging so logs can be queried and transformed into operational signals.
Monitoring for Autoscaling and Reliability Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Observability practice of tracking utilization, saturation, request behavior, and backend dependency health to ensure scaling and resilience mechanisms work correctly.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Autoscaled applications; load-balanced services; backend systems with variable traffic; production systems where capacity issues cause user-visible failures.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Assuming autoscaling works correctly without telemetry or testing under realistic traffic conditions.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better confidence in scaling behavior and failure detection vs. extra setup for metrics, dashboards, and load validation.
💰 Cost / Security
Monitoring scaling signals helps prevent both underprovisioning and overspending. Include backend metrics, not just frontend metrics, because hidden dependency failures often surface as user-facing errors.
💡 Exam Scenario
A service begins returning errors during traffic spikes and the team needs to determine whether the issue comes from load, scaling, or backend dependency health.
Use monitoring dashboards, logs, and alerts to validate autoscaling behavior and backend health signals.
Operations Visibility for Production Readiness Operations & Monitoring

What is it?

Architecture principle that production systems should be observable enough to support troubleshooting, incident response, trend analysis, and service improvement.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Business-critical systems; customer-facing applications; regulated environments; teams practicing continuous improvement and SRE methods.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Deployments that go live with no dashboards, no alerts, limited logs, and no understanding of normal system behavior.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better operational readiness and faster recovery vs. added implementation effort and some telemetry management overhead.
💰 Cost / Security
Production readiness includes dashboards, alerts, logs, escalation ownership, and retention policies. Visibility should be deliberate and secure, not accidental or excessive.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company is preparing a customer-facing service for production and wants to ensure operators can quickly detect, diagnose, and respond to incidents.
Build production readiness around monitoring, logging, and alerting before launch.
