Committed Use Discounts Cost Optimization

What is it?

Billing discount model where you commit to using a certain level of compute resources for one or three years in exchange for lower pricing.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Stable, predictable production workloads with known long-term resource consumption.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Early-stage products, uncertain demand, temporary workloads, and experimentation where future consumption is unknown.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Larger savings than sustained use discounts vs. reduced flexibility because you are committing to future usage.
💰 Cost / Security
Only use committed discounts when demand is predictable and long-lived. Using them too early can increase waste if the workload does not materialize as expected.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company knows a production application will consume a stable baseline of compute for several years and wants to optimize cost aggressively.
Use committed use discounts for the predictable baseline workload.
Free Tier Usage Cost Optimization

What is it?

Limited no-cost usage available for certain Google Cloud services to help teams experiment, learn, and run small workloads economically.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Proofs of concept; learning environments; small startup trials; low-scale experimentation before demand is known.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Production workloads with meaningful scale or business-critical requirements that exceed free-tier limits.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Lowest possible entry cost vs. strict usage limits and unsuitability for larger production needs.
💰 Cost / Security
Free tier is useful for minimizing early cost, but teams must still monitor usage to avoid accidental spend after limits are exceeded. Governance and billing awareness are still required even in trial phases.
💡 Exam Scenario
A small startup is validating product-market fit and wants to minimize cloud costs before customer demand is understood.
Start with free tier options where applicable to reduce initial spend.
Cost Management Training Cost Optimization

What is it?

Organizational practice of teaching teams how to control cloud spend through architecture choices, rightsizing, monitoring, and discount selection.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Growing teams adopting cloud services for the first time; organizations where engineers directly influence resource consumption and architecture cost.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Environments where cost is treated only as a centralized finance issue and engineering teams receive no accountability or visibility.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Broader cost awareness and better engineering decisions vs. time investment for education and process adoption.
💰 Cost / Security
Training helps teams avoid waste from oversized resources, unnecessary always-on services, and poor architectural choices. It is often more scalable than assigning all cost responsibility to a single person.
💡 Exam Scenario
A manager wants startup cloud costs minimized while the team is still learning and future demand is uncertain.
Train the team on service cost management so cost responsibility is built into engineering decisions.
Single Organization with Multi-Project Separation Governance

What is it?

Governance model where one Google Cloud organization contains multiple projects separated by environment, application, or stage.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Enterprises needing centralized identity management with clear separation between development, test, staging, and production resources.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Mixing all environments into one project when separation of duties, billing clarity, or security boundaries are required.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better security boundaries, IAM scoping, and billing isolation vs. more administrative structure and project management overhead.
💰 Cost / Security
Separate projects reduce blast radius and support least privilege by environment. This model also improves cost visibility and governance for production versus non-production workloads.
💡 Exam Scenario
A security team requires least privilege and separation of duties between production and development resources.
Use a single organization and separate projects for each environment or stage of each application.
Environment Separation by Project Governance

What is it?

Practice of placing development, test, staging, and production resources into distinct projects rather than combining them together.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Teams that need IAM isolation, independent billing views, safer testing, and controlled access to production.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Architectures that place all environments into one shared project, increasing the risk of accidental changes and permission sprawl.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Stronger governance and safer operations vs. more setup and cross-project coordination.
💰 Cost / Security
Environment separation supports least privilege, auditability, and reduced operational risk. Production projects should have tighter IAM and change control than development projects.
💡 Exam Scenario
Administrators need separate control of production and development resources while keeping centralized user management.
Create separate projects for production and non-production environments under one organization.
Least Privilege IAM Governance

What is it?

Security model that grants identities only the minimum permissions required to perform their tasks.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
All cloud environments, especially production systems, regulated workloads, and organizations with multiple teams.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Broad owner or editor access granted for convenience, especially across environments with different sensitivity levels.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better security and reduced blast radius vs. more planning and role design effort.
💰 Cost / Security
Least privilege reduces the chance of accidental changes, privilege abuse, and compromise impact. It works best when combined with project separation and clear operational roles.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company mandates least privilege for all infrastructure and wants administration split between production and development.
Apply narrowly scoped IAM roles per project and environment rather than broad shared permissions.
Separation of Duties Governance

What is it?

Administrative design principle where different teams or identities manage different responsibilities, such as development versus production administration.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Enterprises, regulated environments, and organizations that need stronger control over change management and operational risk.
❌ Not a Good Fit
A single unrestricted admin model for all teams across all environments.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better control and auditability vs. more process overhead and cross-team coordination.
💰 Cost / Security
Separation of duties limits the risk of unauthorized or accidental production changes. It also strengthens compliance posture and helps enforce least privilege models.
💡 Exam Scenario
The security team requires separate administration boundaries between production resources and development resources.
Use separate projects and IAM boundaries so production and development administration are handled independently.

Infrastructure Testing Operations

What is it?

Testing practice that validates not only application code but also the deployed cloud infrastructure, configuration, and behavior of managed services.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Cloud-native systems where infrastructure definitions, permissions, networking, autoscaling, and service integrations are critical to application success.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Testing strategies that validate only application logic while assuming infrastructure will always behave correctly in production.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Better end-to-end reliability and deployment confidence vs. more complex test design and operational effort.
💰 Cost / Security
Infrastructure testing can catch IAM, quota, networking, scaling, and deployment misconfigurations before production incidents occur. It is especially important in cloud environments where infrastructure is programmable and dynamic.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company is redesigning test coverage for a new cloud backend and wants to know what should differ from prior platform testing.
Include direct testing of the deployed cloud infrastructure, not just the application code.
Quota and Capacity Checks Operations

What is it?

Operational practice of validating whether service quotas, API limits, or resource caps are preventing scaling or normal service behavior.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Troubleshooting situations where traffic grows suddenly and autoscaling or provisioning does not appear to work as expected.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Assuming every scaling problem is caused only by application bugs without first checking platform limits and backend health.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Fast elimination of platform-level constraints vs. the need for proactive monitoring and quota planning.
💰 Cost / Security
Quotas protect platforms from runaway consumption, but they can also block legitimate growth if not planned properly. Monitoring quotas is a key reliability practice for production systems.
💡 Exam Scenario
A newly popular application feature causes traffic spikes, users see 503 errors, and the system is not scaling properly.
Investigate project quotas and scaling limits early in the troubleshooting process.
Backend Dependency Health Operations

What is it?

Reliability practice of validating whether critical backend services such as databases are healthy and responsive during application incidents.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Multi-tier applications where frontend failures may actually originate from unavailable or overloaded backend dependencies.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Troubleshooting only the frontend tier while ignoring database, cache, or other shared dependencies.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Faster identification of root cause vs. requiring end-to-end observability across service dependencies.
💰 Cost / Security
Backend outages often present to users as timeouts or 503 errors even when web servers appear healthy. Monitoring backend health is essential to maintaining application reliability.
💡 Exam Scenario
Gaming servers are returning 503 errors and slow responses after a popular feature release.
Verify first that the database or other critical backend dependency is online and healthy.

What is it?

Organizational shift where traditional hardware forecasting changes because cloud platforms provide elastic resource provisioning instead of fixed on-premises capacity.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Enterprises moving from data center infrastructure to public cloud operating models.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Organizations expecting cloud adoption to leave all infrastructure planning processes unchanged.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Less dependence on long-term hardware forecasting vs. greater need for quota awareness, financial governance, and architecture-based scaling decisions.
💰 Cost / Security
Cloud reduces the need for some traditional data center expansion planning, but organizations still need usage visibility and cost governance. Capacity planning becomes more dynamic and service-oriented.
💡 Exam Scenario
A legacy enterprise is increasing Google Cloud adoption and wants to know which operational processes will change significantly.
Expect major changes in capacity planning because cloud resources are provisioned differently from physical infrastructure.

What is it?

Financial transition from capital expenditure models for buying hardware to operational expenditure models based on ongoing cloud consumption.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Enterprises moving from owned infrastructure to public cloud services.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Assuming cloud economics behave like traditional up-front hardware procurement.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Greater financial flexibility and pay-as-you-go consumption vs. the need for stronger ongoing spend governance and forecasting discipline.
💰 Cost / Security
Cloud adoption usually changes procurement and finance processes significantly. Teams need better cost visibility, budget controls, and engineering cost awareness.
💡 Exam Scenario
An enterprise is modernizing its infrastructure strategy and wants to know which finance-related legacy process changes most with cloud adoption.
Opex versus capex allocation changes significantly in a cloud operating model.

First-Wave Migration Candidate Selection Migration

What is it?

Strategy of choosing the easiest and lowest-risk applications as the first workloads to move to the cloud.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Organizations beginning cloud adoption and wanting early success with minimal complexity.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Starting with tightly coupled enterprise systems that have many internal dependencies and high migration risk.

Database-First Hybrid Migration Migration

What is it?

Migration pattern where databases are moved to Google Cloud while some applications remain on-premises and continue to interact with them.

↩ click to reveal details

✅ Best Fit
Transitional hybrid architectures; organizations seeking quick progress with manageable application changes; early modernization steps.
❌ Not a Good Fit
Situations where latency between on-premises apps and cloud databases would be unacceptable or where full application migration is immediately feasible and preferred.
⚖ Key Trade-off
Faster partial migration and early cloud adoption vs. hybrid complexity and possible latency or dependency issues.
💰 Cost / Security
Secure connectivity and latency analysis are essential when applications remain on-premises while data moves to the cloud. This approach can be useful as an incremental migration step when chosen carefully.
💡 Exam Scenario
A company wants to deploy applications to the cloud quickly, as-is, and one recommended first step is to move a shared backend dependency while on-premises apps continue using it.
Move appropriate in-house databases first as part of a hybrid migration approach where applications continue to access them.
